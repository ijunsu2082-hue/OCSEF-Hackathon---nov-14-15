## Transmission lines & line ratings

### The Pilot That Never Flew (Stanton, 12 miles from Irvine)

**Asset / event:** SCE EPIC 'Advanced Technology Dynamic Line Rating' pilot at Barre Substation, Stanton, Orange County (2015-2017). The line is not named in the report; the surveyed structures were M3-P4, M3-P7 and M4-P2. Also: CAISO/SCE/SDG&E FERC Order 881 ambient-adjusted-rating deadlines.  
**Operator:** Southern California Edison (pilot); California ISO (Order 881 compliance)  
**Location:** 33.8089, -117.9818 · **4.2 mi** from Westminster HS

Southern California Edison planned a real dynamic line rating pilot that used Nexans/Valley Group CAT-1 tension monitors, with its radio antenna on the tower at Barre Substation in Stanton. The pilot was scheduled to run Dec 2015 to Dec 2016. It was cancelled before any construction because a line-of-sight survey found all three radio paths blocked by vegetation, and SCE concluded the technology was 'not practical' for its system. Ten years later the federal rules have changed. FERC Order 881 requires weather-adjusted (ambient-adjusted) ratings, and FERC gave CAISO until December 17, 2026 and SCE/SDG&E until October 15, 2027. That makes this a 'right now' story for a team pitching software-only line rating that needs no radios on towers. For scale, SCE's alternative is construction: West of Devers cost $740M to triple a corridor from 1,600 MW to 4,800 MW.

**Say on stage:** Twelve miles from this room, Edison tried to let a power line 'breathe' and the project died because trees blocked the radio signal. The federal deadline to do it with weather data instead is December 17, 2026, 33 days from now.

**Facts:**

- [verified] SCE's DLR pilot planned to install CAT-1 tension monitors (load cells, ambient temperature sensor and net radiation sensors) to compute real-time line ratings. Schedule: authority to proceed Jan 2, 2015; pilot operation Dec 19, 2015 to Dec 19, 2016; close-out by Feb 16, 2017. ([source](https://www.sce.com/sites/default/files/inline-files/EPIC_DynamicLineRatingFinalReport_0.pdf))
- [verified] The project was cancelled before equipment was installed. Poles M3-P4, M3-P7 and M4-P2 were surveyed for radio line-of-sight to an antenna on the transmission tower at Barre substation, and all three paths were obstructed by vegetation. ([source](https://www.sce.com/sites/default/files/inline-files/EPIC_DynamicLineRatingFinalReport_0.pdf))
- [verified] SCE's stated problem: its practice is fixed line ratings 'pre-calculated based on assumed-fixed conditions throughout the year'. Its conclusion: DLR 'with the current operating procedure is not practical' because long lines need many radio repeaters. ([source](https://www.sce.com/sites/default/files/inline-files/EPIC_DynamicLineRatingFinalReport_0.pdf))
- [likely] Barre substation is in Stanton (Orange County), at 33.8089, -117.9818 in CEC 2022 substation data. That is 12.4 miles from Irvine by haversine from 33.6846, -117.8265. ([source](https://services.arcgis.com/KkJhFbLnXVqahKz2/arcgis/rest/services/California_Electric_Substations_2022_CEC/FeatureServer))
- [verified] On April 30, 2025, FERC granted CAISO an extension to implement Order 881 (ambient-adjusted line ratings) from June 15, 2025 to December 17, 2026. It also extended the deadline for CAISO participating transmission owners (SCE, SDG&E and others) to October 15, 2027. ([source](https://www.caiso.com/documents/apr-30-2025-order-accepting-motion-for-extension-order-no-881-er22-2362.pdf))
- [verified] CAISO's Sept 1, 2026 release deck lists 'FERC 881 Track 3: AAR in Market Applications & EMS' with the note that the effective date of the Order 881 tariff is 'under internal review'. As of that date, weather-adjusted ratings were not yet live in CAISO's market software. ([source](https://www.caiso.com/documents/presentation-release-user-group-forum-sep-01-2026.pdf))
- [verified] West of Devers Upgrade (SCE, completed May 2021): four 220 kV circuits on a 48-mile corridor between Devers (near Palm Springs), Vista (Grand Terrace) and San Bernardino substations. It raised capacity from 1,600 MW to 4,800 MW at a total cost of $740 million. ([source](https://www.tdworld.com/overhead-transmission/article/21166669/sce-completes-transmission-project-to-support-californias-clean-energy-goals))
- [uncertain] Industry estimates of how much ambient-adjusted ratings add over static ratings vary: about 15-25% (IPS Energy) and 15-40% (Ampacimon, a DLR vendor). EPRI cautions that DLR can come out below legacy ratings a large share of the time where legacy ratings were less conservative. Treat any uplift % in the app as an assumption, not a measured value for SCE lines. ([source](https://ips-energy.com/from-static-to-dynamic-unlocking-hidden-grid-capacity/))

### The 69-kV Choke Point of East County (El Cajon to Los Coches)

**Location:** 34.5049, -117.3884 · **62.9 mi** from Westminster HS

In CAISO's day-ahead market data, the Kramer-Victor 230 kV #2 branch bound in 221 hours in September 2026 and 162 hours in October 2026. Every one of those hours fell between 8 a.m. and 6 p.m.: solar hours, when desert generation tries to squeeze through the wire. Clean energy waits roughly 7 years for steel, while a software rating could start finding headroom in cooler or windier hours now. Be honest on stage: a hot summer noon is close to the worst case, so the biggest gains come on cooler or windy days.

### Eleven Minutes to Dark (Path 44 at San Onofre)

**Asset / event:** Path 44 (five 230 kV lines south of San Onofre) and the SONGS intertie separation scheme: September 8, 2011 Pacific Southwest blackout  
**Operator:** SCE / SDG&E (Path 44 facilities); California ISO (operator of path); event investigated by FERC and NERC  
**Location:** 33.3697, -117.5543 · **37.4 mi** from Westminster HS

On a hot shoulder-season afternoon, September 8, 2011, a technician error tripped the Hassayampa-North Gila 500 kV line in Arizona at 3:27:39 p.m. Power rerouted north through Path 44, the five 230 kV lines at San Onofre, just 27 miles from Irvine. The amps climbed step by step (5,900, 6,700, 7,800) until they passed the 8,000-amp trigger of the San Onofre separation scheme, peaking near 9,500 amps. San Diego was cut off and collapsed, and both San Onofre nuclear units shut down. The cascade lasted about 11 minutes and left about 2.7 million customers without power, including all 1.4 million of SDG&E's. FERC and NERC blamed a lack of real-time awareness of system conditions, which is exactly the visibility a live line-rating dashboard provides. Accuracy note: the 8,000 A figure was a protection-scheme trip threshold, not a thermal sag rating.

**Say on stage:** In 2011, the amps on five wires next to San Onofre crept from 5,900 to 9,500 while nobody was watching, and 2.7 million people went dark in eleven minutes.

**Facts:**

- [verified] Timeline: the Hassayampa-North Gila (H-NG) 500 kV line tripped at 15:27:39. The SONGS separation scheme operated at 15:38:21 and the island collapsed by 15:38:38, about 11 minutes total. H-NG had been at 78% of its normal rating beforehand, on 'a hot, shoulder season day'. ([source](https://cdn.kpbs.org/news/documents/2012/05/01/05-01-12-factsheet-sequence-of-events.pdf))
- [verified] After H-NG tripped, flow on Path 44 rose 84%. Loading went from 5,900 to 6,700 to about 7,800 amps against the 8,000 amps needed to trigger the SONGS separation scheme, then exceeded the trip point and reached as high as 9,500 amps. ([source](https://cdn.kpbs.org/news/documents/2012/05/01/05-01-12-factsheet-sequence-of-events.pdf))
- [verified] Load lost: SDG&E 4,293 MW (about 1.4 million customers); CFE Baja California 2,205 MW (about 1.1 million); IID 929 MW (about 146,000); APS 389 MW (about 70,000). SONGS nuclear units shut down. ([source](https://cdn.kpbs.org/news/documents/2012/05/01/05-01-12-factsheet-sequence-of-events.pdf))
- [verified] The FERC/NERC staff report (released May 1, 2012) says the system was not being operated in a secure N-1 state, a failure stemming primarily from weaknesses in operations planning and real-time situational awareness; about 2.7 million customers lost power. ([source](https://www.ferc.gov/sites/default/files/2020-05/04-27-2012-ferc-nerc-report.pdf))
- [likely] Path 44 consisted of five 230 kV lines south of San Onofre that carried all flows into the San Diego area as well as parts of Arizona and Mexico just before the blackout. ([source](https://www.ferc.gov/sites/default/files/2020-05/20141021101635-IN14-8-000_0.pdf))
- [likely] San Onofre substation coordinates in CEC data are 33.3697, -117.5543, which is 26.8 miles from Irvine. ([source](https://services.arcgis.com/KkJhFbLnXVqahKz2/arcgis/rest/services/California_Electric_Substations_2022_CEC/FeatureServer))
- [uncertain] Open-Meteo ERA5-based reanalysis for San Onofre on 2011-09-08 shows about 28.5-30 C at 12:00-15:00 local with roughly 9-11 km/h wind. These are model estimates, not station observations; use them for the heat-mode replay only with that label. ([source](https://archive-api.open-meteo.com/v1/archive?latitude=33.3697&longitude=-117.5543&start_date=2011-09-08&end_date=2011-09-08&hourly=temperature_2m,wind_speed_10m,shortwave_radiation&timezone=America/Los_Angeles))

REAL: event date 2011-09-08; Path 44 = five 230 kV lines; trip threshold 8,000 A; observed loading 5,900 to 6,700 to 7,800 to 9,500 A; cascade about 11 min (15:27:39 to 15:38:38); load lost SDG&E 4,293 MW, total customers about 2.7M. ## Retired power plants & idle grid connections

### The Ghost Plug at San Onofre

**Asset / event:** San Onofre Nuclear Generating Station (SONGS) Units 2 & 3 and its 230 kV switchyard, plus the planned Imperial Valley-North of SONGS 500 kV line and new North of SONGS substation  
**Operator:** Southern California Edison (78.2% owner; with SDG&E 20% and City of Riverside 1.8%). The switchyard area hosts SDG&E's San Onofre synchronous condenser. The new North of SONGS substation goes to Horizon West Transmission (NextEra) and the 500 kV line to SDG&E (branded the Golden Pacific Powerlink).  
**Location:** 33.36889, -117.555 · **37.4 mi** from Westminster HS

San Onofre was Southern California's largest power plant: 2,246 MW from two reactors. It stopped generating on Jan 31, 2012, after its new steam generators wore out early, and was retired on June 7, 2013. The reactors are being torn down, but the switchyard where it plugged into the grid is one of the few things meant to stay. The state is now spending about $2.28 billion on a 145-mile, 500 kV line from Imperial Valley to a new substation about 10 miles north of SONGS. It will take over the old San Onofre-Santiago and San Onofre-Viejo 230 kV lines, so desert solar can use the corridor the nuclear plant left behind. That is due between 2032 and 2034, so this grid connection will have gone mostly unused for over 20 years.

**Say on stage:** Twenty-six miles from this room is a 2,246-megawatt plug that has been mostly unused since January 2012. The plan to reuse it costs $2.28 billion and won't finish until the 2030s.

**Facts:**

- [verified] SONGS Units 2 and 3 had a combined maximum output of 2,246 MW. They stopped generating on Jan. 31, 2012 and were officially retired on June 7, 2013. ([source](https://www.waterboards.ca.gov/water_issues/programs/ocean/cwa316/saccwis/docs/2026/26saccwisrptf.pdf))
- [likely] Plant coordinates are 33.36889 N, -117.55500 W. Unit 2 entered service in 1983 and Unit 3 in 1984. Ownership: SCE 78.2%, SDG&E 20%, Riverside 1.8%. ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Onofre_Nuclear_Generating_Station))
- [likely] The shutdown came from premature wear in the replacement steam generators. That replacement project cost $671 million and finished in 2011. ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Onofre_Nuclear_Generating_Station))
- [verified] In 2011 SONGS pulled an average of 2,256 million gallons of seawater per day for once-through cooling (EPA ICIS data). The figure was 0 by 2017. ([source](https://www.waterboards.ca.gov/water_issues/programs/ocean/cwa316/saccwis/docs/2026/26saccwisrptf.pdf))

### The Water Plant That Unplugs for the Grid

GWRS is the world's largest potable water recycling plant. ### The Giant Battery That's Been 'Coming Soon' Since 1995

**Asset / event:** Lake Elsinore Advanced Pumped Storage (LEAPS), later renamed the Bluewater Renewable Energy Storage Project. FERC dismissed the license application on Dec 2021 and denied a new preliminary permit on May 18, 2023.  
**Operator:** Nevada Hydro Company (owned by Bluewater Renewable Energy LLC), originally with Elsinore Valley Municipal Water District. Not built.  
**Location:** 33.635, -117.355 · **38.6 mi** from Westminster HS

Pumped storage is a water battery. When solar power is cheap, you pump water uphill. At night or in a heatwave peak, you let it run back down through turbines. LEAPS would use Lake Elsinore as the bottom tank and a new dam in Decker Canyon as the top tank, giving 500 MW for up to 12 hours. That is about a quarter of the old San Onofre nuclear plant, enough to carry a heatwave evening. The idea dates to at least 1995. FERC dismissed the license in December 2021 and in 2023 refused even a new study permit, saying the developer had had years without progress. Lake Hodges in San Diego County shows the concept works at smaller scale: a 40 MW pumped storage plant running since 2012.

**Say on stage:** Twenty-seven miles from here there's a 500-megawatt water battery that could get Orange County through a heatwave evening, and it has been 'proposed' since before most of us were born.

**Facts:**

- [verified] LEAPS would deliver 500 MW for up to 12 hours, with 6,000 MWh of storage. ([source](https://files.cpuc.ca.gov/Environment/info/aspen/nevadahydro/pea5/ch1_exec_summ.pdf))
- [verified] The design: Lake Elsinore as the lower reservoir, a new upper reservoir at Decker Canyon in the Cleveland National Forest, a powerhouse near Grand Avenue, and about 32 miles of 500 kV transmission. ## Heat waves & peak demand

### The Text Message That Saved 2,000 MW

**Asset / event:** CAISO all-time record peak and Energy Emergency Alert 3, September 6, 2022 (pinned at the Santa Ana Fire Station NOAA weather station, the closest official heat gauge to Irvine)  
**Operator:** California ISO (grid operator); Southern California Edison is the local utility in the SCE-TAC area; Cal OES sent the phone alert  
**Location:** 33.7442, -117.8667 · **8.3 mi** from Westminster HS

On the 10-day Labor Day 2022 heat wave (Aug 31 to Sep 9), California's grid hit the highest demand in its history on Sept 6. CAISO declared an Energy Emergency Alert 3, the last step before rolling blackouts. About an hour after the peak, Cal OES sent the first-ever emergency text asking people to cut power, and demand fell by about 2,000 MW within minutes. Batteries, gas and imports covered the rest, and the state avoided rotating outages. The lesson for GridSentinel: the margin between 'fine' and 'blackout' on a heat day was about the size of one text message.

**Say on stage:** On September 6, 2022, California set its all-time power record, and one text message to our phones cut about 2,000 megawatts in minutes. That's like switching off half a million air conditioners at once.

**Facts:**

- [verified] CAISO's official 2022 statistics list a new record peak demand of 52,061 MW on September 6, 2022 at 4:57 p.m.; previous year's peak was 43,982 MW ([source](https://www.caiso.com/Documents/2022Statistics.pdf))
- [verified] EIA and the CPUC Public Advocates Office report the peak as 51,426 MW at 4:55 p.m.; CAISO's own Today's Outlook 5-minute demand file shows the same 51,426 MW at 16:55. Two sourced numbers exist; use 52,061 MW when quoting CAISO's official record and note the 51,426 MW 5-minute value ([source](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=54039))
- [verified] The previous all-time CAISO record was 50,270 MW on July 24, 2006 ([source](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=54039))
- [verified] CAISO issued an Energy Emergency Alert Stage 3 shortly after the peak; the previous EEA3 was in August 2020 ([source](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=54039))
- [verified] Within five minutes of the Cal OES wireless emergency alert, demand fell more than 2,100 MW below CAISO's hour-ahead forecast and stayed below forecast until 9 p.m. ([source](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=54039))

### The Renewable Highway That Went Underground in Chino Hills

**Asset / event:** Tehachapi Renewable Transmission Project (TRTP), Segment 8 500 kV underground section through Chino Hills and Chino  
**Operator:** Southern California Edison (SCE)  
**Location:** 33.98, -117.72 · **22.9 mi** from Westminster HS

SCE built TRTP, a 173-mile chain of new and upgraded 500 kV lines and substations, to carry up to 4,500 MW of mostly wind power from the Tehachapi area into the LA Basin. In Chino Hills, about 21 miles from Irvine, SCE had already put up overhead towers when residents fought them. In 2013 the CPUC ordered that stretch rebuilt underground. SCE demolished 16 poles and towers and installed North America's first 500 kV XLPE underground cable, roughly 3.5 to 3.7 miles long, commissioned in Oct 2016. The full line was energized in Dec 2016. Building new transmission is slow, disputed, and expensive, which is why getting more out of existing wires matters.

**Say on stage:** Twenty-one miles from Irvine, the line built to bring Tehachapi wind into Southern California had to have its towers torn down and be buried under Chino Hills.

**Facts:**

- [verified] SCE: TRTP can carry 4,500 MW. It comprises about 250 miles of transmission facilities across about 173 miles in 11 segments, and was fully energized in Q4 / December 2016. SCE says it serves the equivalent of about 3 million homes. Undergrounding of Segment 8 in Chino Hills began construction in summer 2014. ([source](https://www.sce.com/about-sce/reliability/projects-in-progress/tehachapi-renewable-transmission-project))
- [verified] T&D World (Dec 19, 2016): the underground segment is 'the first North American installation of 500-kV XLPE transmission line' and the world's first 500 kV line in duct structures along steep terrain. Cables were commissioned Oct 2016, and 16 double-circuit poles and towers plus 27 foundations of the partially built overhead line were demolished. The segment uses two transition stations of about 3 acres each and is about 3.7 miles long per the CPUC ruling. ([source](https://www.tdworld.com/grid-innovations/distribution/article/20967561/sce-puts-500-kv-underground))
- [verified] The CPUC granted Chino Hills' petition in 2013 and ordered SCE to replace the partially built 500 kV overhead line with underground facilities (CPUC addendum June 2013, about 3.5 miles). ([source](https://www.tdworld.com/home/article/20969222/california-puc-approves-undergrounding-of-tehachapi-transmission-line-through-chino-hills))
- [uncertain] The pin coordinates (33.98, -117.72) are an approximate point in Chino Hills, not a surveyed transition-station location. The underground cost was reported as about $224 million in a pre-construction estimate, but we could not tie that figure to a specific primary source, so do not use it on stage. ([source](https://chinohills.org/index.aspx?nid=1115))

**Map node values:** Line node or polyline: capacity 4,500 MW (real: SCE), 500 kV (real), energized Dec 2016 (real), underground length about 3.5-3.7 mi (real), first North American 500 kV XLPE (real). Line loading or current flow: no public real-time data, so use an ESTIMATE or a DLR-simulated value. Upgrade potential: DLR on overhead TRTP segments is a hypothetical ESTIMATE, since no SCE DLR deployment on TRTP was found. Note: underground cable cannot be cooled by wind, so DLR applies only to the overhead segments.

**Team bullets covered:** Capacity (4,500 MW corridor), infrastructure age (2016, newest major line), upgrade potential (DLR on existing overhead lines vs new construction), output and power draw (renewable flow into the LA Basin).
